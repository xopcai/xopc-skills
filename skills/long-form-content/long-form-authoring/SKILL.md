# Long Form Authoring

Establish the reader, desired change in understanding, genre, approximate length and available evidence. Start with the material already supplied. Ask only for missing facts that block a faithful draft; use explicit assumptions for reversible editorial choices.

## Build the manuscript spine
Create a central promise, chapter sequence and scope for each chapter. Map claims and examples to source IDs with page or section locations. Keep a small continuity ledger of terms, chronology, named entities, units, definitions and unresolved questions. Distinguish supplied evidence, verified external evidence and proposed interpretation.

For manuals, organize around reader tasks, prerequisites, steps, expected results and recovery paths. For white papers, connect problem, evidence, options, tradeoffs and recommendations. For books or narrative reports, preserve argument progression and chronology without manufacturing scenes or interviews.

## Draft and revise across chapters
Draft coherent sections at a time while maintaining the overall target length. Reuse definitions by reference rather than re-explaining them in every chapter. Keep factual gaps visibly marked as questions for the author. Do not invent quotations, statistics, sources, lived experiences or customer testimonials to make the prose flow.

When a source changes, find affected claims, tables, examples and later conclusions before updating the manuscript. Resolve contradictions openly; never choose the more convenient number silently. Retain uncertainty when a disagreement cannot be resolved. Honor requests for an entire manuscript by continuing through all agreed chapters; an outline is not the finished deliverable.

## Deliver
Check coverage against the chapter plan, term consistency, duplicate passages, transitions, source support and open questions. Provide the editable manuscript, source ledger and a concise note on unresolved factual or editorial decisions. If length or source limits prevent completion, state exactly which chapters are complete.

Use an available document capability only when the user requests a formatted artifact. Do not silently publish, submit, impersonate an author or reproduce substantial copyrighted source passages.

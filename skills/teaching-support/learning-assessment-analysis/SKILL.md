# Learning Assessment Analysis

Confirm the assessment purpose, learners, curriculum scope, item-to-objective mapping, scoring rules, accommodations, missing submissions, and whether comparisons are appropriate. De-identify student-level data in broad reports.

Validate totals, score ranges, duplicate records, absent versus zero, item keys, partial credit, and version differences. Report distribution and objective-level performance rather than relying only on the mean. Separate item difficulty, discrimination, missingness, and possible ambiguity from claims about learner ability.

Identify class-wide gaps, subgroup patterns only when ethically and statistically justified, and individual follow-up needs without fixed labels. Cross-check test signals with student work or classroom evidence. A single assessment does not establish motivation, intelligence, or learning style.

Deliver data-quality notes, assessment summary, objective/item analysis, misconceptions supported by response evidence, intervention groups defined by current need, reteach/enrichment actions, and a reassessment plan. Keep named results restricted and require teacher review for consequential decisions.

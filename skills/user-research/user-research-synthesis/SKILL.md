# User Research Synthesis

Confirm the research question, decision to inform, target population, collection method, sample, time window, product version, and known biases. Inventory every source and protect participant identity; replace names and contact details with stable participant IDs in working outputs.

## Prepare the evidence

Separate direct observation, participant statement, researcher interpretation, and product telemetry. Retain source pointers such as participant ID plus timestamp, survey item, ticket ID, or event query. Note recruitment, non-response, moderation, translation, survivorship, and instrumentation limitations.

Normalize the corpus without erasing disagreement. Code needs, jobs, behaviors, triggers, barriers, workarounds, emotions, outcomes, and context. Use an initial codebook, revise it when the data demands, and keep a short change log so another reviewer can understand the grouping.

## Synthesize, then quantify carefully

Cluster recurring evidence into themes and counter-themes. For each theme include supporting cases, disconfirming cases, affected segments, severity, frequency within the observed sample, and confidence. Say `6 of 12 interviewed participants` rather than implying market prevalence.

Triangulate qualitative themes with survey or behavior data only when definitions, populations, and time windows are compatible. Identify contradictions explicitly. A quote illustrates a theme; it does not prove it.

Translate strong themes into insight statements that connect situation, observed behavior, underlying need, consequence, and evidence. Then frame opportunity areas or hypotheses without jumping directly to a favored feature.

Deliver:

- research scope, sample, and limitations;
- source inventory and coding summary;
- ranked themes with evidence and counter-evidence;
- segment differences and a current-state journey when supported;
- insight and opportunity statements;
- unanswered questions and a next-research plan;
- an appendix mapping each conclusion to its sources.

Do not expose raw participant data broadly, infer sensitive traits, erase minority experiences, or report statistical certainty unsupported by the research design.

# Evidence protocol

## Source order

1. Direct observation: local code/data, official filings, specifications, contracts, product behavior.
2. Primary authority: regulator, standards body, official documentation, original research, first-party dataset.
3. Strong secondary analysis: named experts or institutions with disclosed methods and direct citations.
4. Discovery signals: market listings, search snippets, forums, social posts, anonymous commentary.

Lower tiers can reveal questions and lived experience. They cannot independently prove a consequential claim when a higher-tier source should exist.

## Confidence

- High: direct observation or multiple independent authoritative sources with matching scope.
- Medium: one authoritative source, or multiple credible secondary sources with matching definitions.
- Low: one indirect source, unresolved conflict, weak method, stale data, or incomplete scope.

## Independence check

Two pages repeating the same press release are one source. A marketplace listing and the publisher's landing page are not independent. Record common origin when detectable.

## Contradiction check

Before choosing between conflicting claims, compare: definition, population, geography, period, unit, denominator, method, publication date, incentives, and whether one source cites the other.

## Quantitative claims

Retain raw values, unit, period, denominator, formula, result, source, and caveat. Do not compare downloads with active users, list price with realized cost, or benchmark results from different workloads without an explicit normalization.

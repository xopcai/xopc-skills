# Find Community

Start from the founder's real access: work history, interests, trusted relationships, languages, locations, online groups and offline networks. Ask for evidence of participation and repeated problems. Never treat a demographic label or a large audience as a community by itself.

Create a candidate ledger with: community boundary, founder credibility, gathering places, recurring jobs or complaints, current workarounds, access path, willingness-to-pay signals, conflicts or ethical risks, and evidence date. Separate observations from assumptions. Use current research only when needed and preserve sources; do not scrape restricted or private spaces.

Score candidates on authentic access, problem frequency and severity, ability to reach ten relevant people, observable spending or costly workarounds, founder motivation, and competitive room. Reject communities that require identity deception, unsolicited extraction of member data, or expertise the founder cannot responsibly claim.

Return the top one to three communities, evidence and confidence, specific unknowns, ten interview targets described by role or relationship rather than invented identities, and a seven-day participation-and-learning plan. End with a recommendation to explore, gather more evidence, or reject—without proposing a product prematurely.

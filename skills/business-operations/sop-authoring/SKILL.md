# SOP Authoring

Identify the process owner, performers, trigger, end state, frequency, systems, inputs, outputs, controls, exceptions, and evidence of how the work is actually done. Separate current practice, approved policy, and proposed improvement.

Map the process before writing prose. For each step specify responsible role, prerequisite, action, system or artifact, expected result, evidence captured, time or quality limit, and failure route. Use decision points where outcomes differ; do not hide judgment inside vague verbs such as handle or follow up.

Include purpose and scope, definitions, roles, prerequisites, ordered procedure, controls, exceptions and escalation, records, checklist, version owner, effective date, and review cadence. Keep credentials and sensitive values out of the SOP.

Walk through at least one normal case, one exception, and one handoff with a representative user. Flag steps that cannot be performed, measured, or evidenced. Deliver the draft, unresolved policy questions, RACI or role map when needed, test record, change log, and rollout/training plan. Publishing or replacing a controlled procedure requires owner approval.

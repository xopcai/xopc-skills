# Month End Close Review

Identify the legal entity, close period, checklist version, materiality rule, evidence cutoff and accountable reviewers. Use the organization's supplied accounting policy. Do not invent recognition rules or infer that a generic checklist is a statutory requirement.

## Assemble the evidence register
For each close task, record owner, dependency, due date, source file and location, expected control, preparer state, reviewer state and outstanding exceptions. Distinguish not started, evidence missing, prepared, reviewed and blocked. A ticked box or an empty exception list is not evidence of review.

Where data permits, recompute trial-balance debit/credit totals, subledger-to-control-account balances, bank-to-book reconciliations and opening-plus-movements-to-closing balances. Check sign conventions, currency and period. Keep deposits in transit and outstanding payments as reconciling items; do not erase them to force a match.

Review cutoff-sensitive items using supplied receiving, service, invoice and posting dates. Record discrepancies as questions for the accountant rather than automatically posting accruals, reversing entries or deciding tax treatment. Proposed adjustments must retain source, rationale, amount, period and reviewer-needed status.

## Surface blockers and handover
Prioritize material unreconciled balances, missing approvals, unresolved dependencies and late evidence. Separate arithmetic agreement from accounting appropriateness. A balanced trial balance does not prove every entry is correct.

Deliver a close readiness matrix, reconciliation calculations, an exception log and a handover list with owner and next evidence required. Identify which checks could not be run and which were actually recomputed. Do not label the period closed, audited or compliant without the applicable recorded sign-off. Do not post journal entries, lock periods, submit returns or send sensitive workpapers unless separately requested and authorized.

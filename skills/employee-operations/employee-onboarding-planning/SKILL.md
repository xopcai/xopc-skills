# Employee Onboarding Planning

Use the confirmed role, start date, work location, manager and supplied company procedures. Identify what is already decided and what still needs an owner. Do not repeat recruitment evaluation or infer sensitive personal traits.

## Build a dependency-aware plan
Separate pre-start preparation, first-day essentials, first-week learning and later role milestones. Use 30/60/90-day checkpoints only when they fit the role and contract period. Assign each task an owner, due date or start-relative offset, dependency, expected evidence and status.

Include equipment, access requests, orientation, role-specific training, buddy/manager contact and first deliverable only as supported by the role and available procedures. Account creation, privileged access and payroll entry belong to authorized HR/IT processes. Record requests and approvers rather than treating a plan as permission to provision.

Collect the minimum administrative data required. Prefer a status such as identity documents submitted through HR's secure channel over embedding ID scans, bank accounts, health details or family data in the shared plan. For accommodations, use the employee's voluntarily supplied work needs and an appropriate private owner; do not diagnose or circulate sensitive details.

## Make readiness and learning visible
Distinguish completion evidence from checked boxes. If equipment or account approval is delayed, show dependent tasks as blocked and offer feasible non-sensitive learning work. Set observable learning or delivery milestones and feedback dates; avoid unsupported productivity quotas or automated probation decisions.

Deliver the plan, responsibility matrix, missing inputs and a short manager/buddy handover. Keep proposed invitations and welcome messages as drafts unless sending was explicitly requested. Do not make employment decisions, change compensation, sign documents or silently write HR systems.

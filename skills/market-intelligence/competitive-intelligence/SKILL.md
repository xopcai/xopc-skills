# Competitive Intelligence

Confirm the decision, market and geography, customer segment, comparison date, your offering, candidate competitors, and dimensions that matter. Classify direct, indirect, substitute, and benchmark competitors rather than comparing famous names by default.

Build a source ledger. Prefer current product documentation, pricing and terms, release notes, filings, and official announcements for what a company claims; use credible independent reporting, customer evidence, and win/loss data for external validation. Date volatile facts and distinguish public, internal, inferred, and unknown information.

Normalize packaging, units, contract terms, audience, and feature definitions before comparison. A feature name is not evidence of equivalent capability. Record test conditions for firsthand product checks and preserve meaningful disadvantages of your own offering.

Deliver competitor profiles, an evidence-linked comparison matrix, changes since the prior review, where each option fits or fails, implications for the stated decision, unanswered questions, and monitoring signals. For sales use, provide honest discovery questions and proof points—not manipulative traps or unsupported attack claims. Never misrepresent identity, bypass access controls, solicit confidential information, or present stale pricing as current.

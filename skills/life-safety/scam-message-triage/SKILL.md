# Scam Message Triage

Ask what arrived, through which channel, what the sender requests, whether the user clicked, installed, shared a code, enabled screen sharing, or transferred funds, and what evidence can be safely preserved. Redact identity, account, card, and authentication values from shared material.

Treat urgency, secrecy, guaranteed returns, advance fees, remote-control software, screen sharing, unknown QR codes, off-platform payment, credential or verification-code requests, and transfers to a so-called safe account as strong risk signals. A familiar logo, caller ID, account name, or accurate personal detail does not establish authenticity.

Do not open or fetch a suspicious link, scan its QR code, call a number in the message, reply, install software, or test a payment. Verify by independently navigating to the organization's official app/site or using a number from a trusted statement or government source.

If exposure occurred, prioritize stopping contact and payment, disconnecting remote access, contacting the bank/platform through an independent channel, changing affected credentials from a clean device, preserving screenshots and transaction records, and contacting local police or official anti-fraud services. Do not destroy evidence or pay a recovery service.

Deliver a risk level with evidence, unknowns, immediate actions, independent verification steps, and an evidence checklist. Phrase conclusions as likely/uncertain unless authoritative verification exists.

# Presentation Deck

Define audience, presentation setting, decision or learning outcome, duration, evidence, and whether an existing template is authoritative. Build a narrative before laying out slides: context, tension or question, evidence, decision or insight, and next action. One slide should make one primary point.

Use the supplied brand and template when present. Otherwise choose a restrained visual system with intentional typography, spacing, color, and chart style. Vary layouts according to content rather than decorating repeated text boxes. Prefer diagrams, charts, timelines, annotated visuals, and concise evidence over dense prose. Preserve source attribution and do not turn estimates into facts.

When editing, preserve masters, theme, notes, hidden slides, animations, links, and accessibility metadata unless the request changes them. After generation, reopen the deck, verify slide order and editable objects, render every slide, and inspect overflow, overlap, contrast, font substitution, chart labels, image quality, alignment, and consistency. Deliver the deck plus a short content and visual QA summary.
